## 2. Personal Data We Collect

The personal data we collect depends on how you use the Platform, the features you access, the transactions you perform and the information you choose to provide.

### 2.1 Account and registration data

When you register for or use the Platform, we may collect:

- Full name.
- Email address and mobile number.
- Account identifiers and authentication information.
- Passwords, which are intended to be stored in hashed form rather than as readable passwords.
- Organisation name, business address, designation and role.
- Information about your authority to represent an organisation.
- Account preferences, subscription information and account status.

### 2.2 Identity verification and KYC data

Where identity verification is required for account approval, access to restricted features, trading or compliance purposes, we may collect:

- Details appearing on government-issued identity documents.
- Photographs or scans of identity documents.
- Name, date of birth, address or other information appearing on the submitted documents, where required for verification.
- Organisation registration documents and relevant corporate identification details.
- Details of directors, authorised representatives or authorised signatories where necessary.
- KYC review status, verification outcomes, reviewer notes and records of requests for additional information.

**KYC is reviewed by authorised EtherTrack employees.** We do not use an external identity verification agency to perform KYC review as part of our stated process.

KYC information is used to verify identity, assess eligibility, prevent impersonation and fraud, enforce Platform requirements and meet applicable legal obligations.

Please do not submit identity information that is unrelated to the verification purpose. Where you use an Aadhaar document, submit a masked copy displaying only the last four digits, where accepted for the relevant verification process. Do not submit Aadhaar biometrics.

We do not claim to perform Aadhaar authentication or collect Aadhaar biometric information through the Platform.

### 2.3 Emissions, environmental and organisational data

When using the Platform's emissions management, reporting or carbon-credit features, you may provide:

- Scope 1, Scope 2 and Scope 3 emissions information.
- Energy consumption, fuel consumption, operational activity and other environmental data.
- Supplier, facility, project and organisational information.
- Supporting invoices, bills, spreadsheets, evidence documents and uploaded records.
- Emission factors, calculation assumptions, reporting boundaries and related methodological information.
- Sustainability disclosures and information used to prepare environmental reports.
- Information about suppliers, employees, authorised representatives or other individuals included in uploaded records.

Organisations are responsible for ensuring that they have the necessary authority to provide personal data relating to their employees, suppliers, customers or other individuals.

### 2.4 Carbon-credit and transaction data

When you use carbon-credit portfolio, tokenisation, marketplace or retirement features, we may process:

- Carbon-credit project and registry-related information.
- Credit identifiers, quantities, ownership and portfolio records.
- Listings, orders, offers, trade history and transaction details.
- Retirement instructions and retirement records.
- Connected blockchain wallet addresses.
- Public blockchain transaction hashes, token identifiers and related transaction metadata.
- Records required to investigate disputes, suspected misuse or transaction irregularities.

Certain transaction information may be publicly visible when recorded on a public blockchain.

### 2.5 Communications and support data

When you contact us, we may collect your email address, contact details, support requests, feedback, attachments and communications with our team.

We may retain records of complaints, requests, disputes and the actions taken to resolve them.

### 2.6 Technical and usage data

When you visit or use the Platform, we may automatically collect:

- IP address and connection information.
- Browser type, operating system and device information.
- Pages or features accessed and timestamps.
- Authentication events, session information and security events.
- Error reports, diagnostic information and performance logs.
- Usage information collected through analytics technologies, where enabled with the required consent.

### 2.7 Cookies and similar technologies

We use cookies and similar technologies for authentication, security, session management and, where you consent, analytics.

Further details are provided in Section 7.

### 2.8 Payment data

Payments are processed through Razorpay or its applicable payment infrastructure.

Depending on the transaction, EtherTrack may receive payment references, transaction amounts, payment status, timestamps, billing information and the type of payment method used.

We do not ordinarily receive or store your complete card number, card security code (CVV), UPI PIN or net-banking password through our Platform. Payment credentials are handled through the applicable payment provider's systems.

### 2.9 Information we do not intentionally collect

We do not intentionally request health information, precise location information or biometric identifiers for ordinary Platform operations.

If such information is incidentally included in a document or communication you submit, we will handle it in accordance with applicable law and the purpose for which the information was provided.

## 6. Blockchain, IPFS and Identity Documents

EtherTrack uses blockchain-related infrastructure to support carbon-credit and transaction features. Blockchain technology creates specific privacy and deletion limitations that users should understand before transacting.

### 6.1 Public and persistent blockchain records

Transactions recorded on a public blockchain may be visible to anyone, replicated across independent network nodes and difficult or impossible to alter or delete.

Such records may include wallet addresses, transaction hashes, token identifiers, quantities and transaction timestamps.

Wallet addresses are generally pseudonymous rather than directly identifying. However, an address may become associated with an identifiable person through other information, transaction patterns or public disclosures.

### 6.2 Personal data and blockchain

EtherTrack does not intend to record names, email addresses, mobile numbers, government identity documents or other direct personal identifiers on public blockchains.

Nevertheless, blockchain metadata, wallet addresses or transaction records may constitute personal data where they can be linked to an identifiable individual.

Users should not assume that pseudonymous information is necessarily anonymous.

### 6.3 Identity document storage

KYC documents are reviewed by authorised EtherTrack employees.

We use access restrictions and applicable storage protections to limit access to identity documents. Where encrypted document storage is implemented, documents are encrypted using the applicable technical controls.

Where IPFS is used, a content identifier (CID) or cryptographic hash may be used to reference content. A hash does not itself guarantee anonymity, and a CID may enable access to content if the referenced content is publicly available or otherwise accessible.

We do not intend to publish readable identity documents on a public blockchain or make KYC documents publicly accessible through the Platform.

### 6.4 Deletion limitations

You may request deletion of personal data held by EtherTrack, subject to applicable legal retention requirements.

Where deletion is permitted, we will delete or anonymise the relevant stored information and remove references under our control, as technically and legally feasible.

We cannot directly erase blockchain records maintained by independent network participants. Similarly, copies or records outside our control may not be capable of immediate deletion.

Where these limitations affect your request, we will explain the relevant limitations and the actions taken in response.

### 6.5 Transaction responsibility

Before connecting a wallet or authorising a blockchain transaction, you should understand that public blockchain activity may be permanent and visible beyond the Platform.

Never provide your wallet's private key or seed phrase to EtherTrack or any person claiming to represent us.

## 9. Data Retention and Deletion

We retain personal data only for as long as reasonably necessary for the purposes described in this Policy, subject to applicable legal, accounting, regulatory, security and dispute-resolution requirements.

Our intended retention approach is as follows:

### 9.1 Account and KYC records

Account and KYC information is retained while an account is active and for the additional period reasonably necessary for verification records, fraud prevention, dispute resolution and applicable legal or regulatory obligations.

A five-year period after account closure may be used for relevant records where justified by the applicable retention requirements and business purpose. It is not intended to override a shorter mandatory deletion requirement or justify retaining every data field without a continuing purpose.

### 9.2 Transaction, invoice and accounting records

Transaction, invoice and accounting records are retained for the period required by applicable corporate, tax and accounting laws.

Where an eight-financial-year retention requirement applies to the relevant books of account under the Companies Act, 2013, we will retain those records for the prescribed period. This does not automatically extend the same period to every category of personal data.

### 9.3 Platform content

Emissions data, supporting evidence, reports and other user-created content are retained while needed to provide the relevant service.

Following account closure, users may be given an opportunity to export eligible information in accordance with our Terms of Service. After the applicable export period, content that is no longer required will be deleted or anonymised within a reasonable period, subject to legal retention requirements, backups and technical limitations.

Our intended operational target for eligible content is generally within 90 days after the relevant deletion or closure process, where technically feasible.

### 9.4 Support communications

Support records are retained for a period appropriate to the nature of the request, the need to maintain service history, and any applicable dispute or legal requirements. An operational retention period of up to three years after closure of a support matter may be used where justified.

### 9.5 Technical and error logs

Technical and error logs are retained for security, troubleshooting and operational monitoring. Our intended ordinary retention period is up to 90 days, unless a longer period is required to investigate a security incident or comply with law.

### 9.6 Analytics data

Analytics information is retained according to the applicable analytics configuration and consent settings. Our intended retention period is up to 14 months, subject to the settings and retention controls available in the relevant service.

### 9.7 Blockchain records

Public blockchain records may remain permanently available. EtherTrack cannot guarantee their deletion from independent blockchain networks.

### 9.8 Applying retention rules

The periods above are operational targets or applicable legal periods, as indicated. They are not a representation that every category of data must or will be retained for the same duration.

When personal data is no longer required and no applicable legal obligation requires retention, we will delete or anonymise it, subject to legitimate technical limitations and applicable law.

## 10. Information Security and Data Breaches

### 10.1 Security safeguards

We seek to protect personal data through reasonable technical and organisational safeguards appropriate to the nature of the information and the risks associated with its processing.

Depending on the relevant system and data category, safeguards may include:

- HTTPS/TLS encrypted connections.
- Password hashing and secure authentication practices.
- Role-based access controls and restrictions on access to KYC information.
- Protection of stored documents and sensitive operational data.
- Security logging, monitoring and incident investigation.
- Controls over service-provider access and integrations.
- Measures intended to prevent unauthorised access, alteration, disclosure or destruction of personal data.

These safeguards are not a guarantee that every attack, vulnerability or data breach can be prevented.

### 10.2 Employee access

KYC documents are reviewed by authorised EtherTrack employees. Access to identity documents and related review records should be limited to personnel who need that access for verification, compliance, security or authorised operational purposes.

Employees and other authorised persons are expected to handle such information confidentially and in accordance with applicable internal controls and legal obligations.

### 10.3 User responsibilities

You are responsible for protecting your account credentials, maintaining the confidentiality of your authentication information and securing your connected wallet.

Do not share your password, private key, seed phrase or authentication codes with unauthorised persons. EtherTrack will never ask you to disclose your wallet's private key or seed phrase.

Notify us promptly if you suspect unauthorised access to your account or a security incident involving your information.

### 10.4 Personal data breaches and cybersecurity incidents

If we identify a personal data breach or cybersecurity incident, we will assess its nature and impact, take appropriate containment and remediation measures, preserve relevant evidence where necessary, and notify affected individuals and authorities within the timelines and in the manner required by applicable law.

This includes applicable CERT-In reporting requirements under the Information Technology Act, 2000 and the CERT-In directions, as well as applicable breach-notification obligations under the DPDP Act and DPDP Rules when the relevant provisions take effect.

CERT-In directions include a six-hour reporting requirement for covered cybersecurity incidents. Applicable reporting deadlines will be assessed independently rather than assuming that one deadline applies to every incident.
